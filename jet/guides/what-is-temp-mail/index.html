What Is Temp Mail? How a Disposable Email Works

Published November 21, 2026, 5 min read

Temp mail is an email address you use for a short time and then forget. It receives real messages, verification codes and confirmation links, but it is not tied to your name, your phone number or your main inbox. You open a page, an address is already waiting, and you paste it wherever a form asks for an email.

People also call it a disposable email, a throwaway address or a burner email. The idea is the same in every case: give a website something that works today, without handing over the address you will still be using in ten years.

How does a disposable email address actually work?

A temp mail service owns one or more domain names, like jetboxmail.com. It accepts every message sent to any name on that domain and shows it on a web page. When you open the site, it simply picks a random name for you, for example k7xq2mfa9p@jetboxmail.com, and watches for mail sent to it.

There is no account to create, because the address does not need one. The service never sends anything from it either: it only receives. That is a big part of why these addresses exist. They are a mailbox with a front door and no back door.

When is a temp mail address worth using?

Any time a website wants your email for something you do not plan to keep. A few typical cases:

  • Downloading a free guide, a template or a white paper that sits behind a form.
  • Trying an app or an online tool before you decide whether you like it.
  • Getting onto hotel, airport or café Wi-Fi that asks for an email first.
  • Entering a giveaway, or reading one article behind a "sign up to continue" wall.
  • Testing your own website or newsletter signup form if you are a developer.

In all of these, the site gets a working address, you get your code or your link, and the marketing emails that follow go somewhere you will never look.

When should you use your real address instead?

This is the part most temp mail sites skip, and it matters. A disposable address is the wrong tool for anything you may need to get back into later. If you lose the address, you also lose the way to reset the password.

Is anyone else able to read my temp mail?

It depends on the kind of address. Without an account, a temp mail inbox is public in practice: anyone who types the exact same address on the site can see its messages. That is why random names are safer than short ones like john@, and why you should never send anything private to a public inbox.

How long do the messages stay?

Short lives are the point of temp mail, so messages do not stay forever. Here is how it works on this site:

Why do some websites refuse disposable emails?

Some services keep lists of known temp mail domains and block them at signup. They do it to limit fake accounts and free trial abuse. It is not personal, and there is no trick worth fighting it with. You have two honest options: switch to the other domain, which a site may not have on its list, or use your real address for that one service.

Can I send email from a temp mail address?

No, and that is on purpose. An address that can send mail without any account would be a gift to spammers and scammers. Jetbox Mail addresses only receive. If you need to reply to someone, use an inbox you own.

Frequently asked questions

Is temp mail free?

Yes. You get an address the moment the page opens, with no sign-up and no payment. A free account adds one private address. Premium is optional and only matters if you want many addresses, longer storage or forwarding.

Is temp mail legal?

Using a disposable address to protect your privacy is legal. Using any email address for fraud, harassment or to break a website's rules is not, and the address will not hide you from the consequences.

What is the difference between temp mail and an email alias?

An alias forwards to your real inbox, so it is tied to you and lasts. Temp mail is separate from your real inbox and is meant to be dropped. Aliases suit long-term accounts; temp mail suits quick, one-time signups.
